Misprints and error cards

A printing or production error can make a card unusual, but first establish what you're seeing. Wear, alterations, and counterfeits need different checks.

What might be an error?

Examples include a cut that exposes part of a neighboring card or a front and back printed out of alignment. Compare with the photographs in CGC's error-card guide; appearance and terminology matter.

Common error types

  • Miscuts: The cut is shifted enough to show a neighboring card or other unintended parts of the sheet.
  • Misaligned printing: The front, back, or a printed layer does not line up correctly.
  • Extra or missing ink: Unintended ink marks or missing color change part of the design.
  • Square-cut corners: One or more corners missed the usual rounding step.
  • Holo errors: A misplaced, doubled, or otherwise unintended foil pattern.

Uneven borders or an unusual mark alone are not enough to conclude that a card has a valuable error.

What to do next

  1. Identify the regular card. Search its name and number in the Catalog Explorer and compare the reference image.
  2. Photograph the front, back, and unusual area clearly. Keep any packaging and purchase details.
  3. Review condition and authenticity before describing it as an error.
  4. Ask for a second opinion. r/PokemonMisprints can help with identification; treat community comments as opinions, not authentication or an appraisal.

Protect the card as it is. When estimating value, look for sales of comparable confirmed errors and use the regular card's price only as a starting reference. Don't assume an unusual feature automatically adds a premium.
