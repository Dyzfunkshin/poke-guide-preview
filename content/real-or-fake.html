Real or fake?

If a card looks wrong, pause before pricing, buying, or selling it. Compare several details together; a single photograph or unusual feature may not settle authenticity.

Compare the exact card

  1. Confirm its identity. Follow the identification steps, then search the Catalog Explorer by name or number. Match the language, set, artwork, and printing.
  2. Read the print. Compare the name, card number, wording, symbols, and text placement with a reliable reference for that release.
  3. Inspect both sides. Look at the print quality, back design, borders, and any foil or texture. Compare the same printing; different releases can look different.
  4. Check the purchase context. Ask for clear photographs of the actual card or sealed product, and review the seller's history and return terms.

Keep checks non-destructive. Don't rip, bend, burn, scrape, or peel a card to test it.

Still unsure?

Ask an experienced card shop to inspect it in person. Pokémon Support's counterfeit guidance also recommends seeking a specialist shop's opinion and comparing official product images.

If the card seems authentic but has an unusual production feature, check misprints and error cards. For loose-pack and box buying considerations, see Japanese packs. Once you're comfortable with the identification, move on to condition and value.
